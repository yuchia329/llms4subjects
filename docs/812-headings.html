812 headings no document carries

Every published system on this benchmark proposes subject headings by document similarity: it finds a record's nearest neighbours in a labelled corpus and harvests their headings. 812 of the gold test split's headings appear on no document in any corpus the shared task releases, so they cannot be harvested from a neighbour at any k, from any index, however good the encoder. They carry 7.2% of the split's gold assignments — unreachable by the entire family of methods the leaderboard is made of, including the one that won it.

The zero-shot band, one square per heading

992 headings in the gold test split occur in no tib-core training record. Growing the index from 32,043 documents to all 70,579 the shared task releases — a second track, 38,545 extra labelled records — reaches 180 of them. Every one of those 180 lands in the tail: one to nine documents each, enough to be proposed in principle and rarely enough to be ranked.

Where the hits fall

34.1% of the split carries exactly one gold heading, and this system reaches 0.7367 R@10 on those records against 0.4163 on records with five or more. It finds a correct heading more often than the published systems and all of a record's headings less often. The aggregate the leaderboard is read on rewards the first.

Aggregation before method

One set of predictions scores 0.5910 record-micro, 0.7156 one-vote-per-cell across all 20 cells, and 0.6299 across the 9 cells the organizers' scorer can read. Aggregation alone moves it 0.1245 at k=10, where 0.08 separates the best published R@10 from the worst of the four. A cell holding one German conference volume gets the same vote as one holding 1,465 German books, and eleven of twenty cells raise a KeyError inside the organizers' reader.

How it is built, briefly

The pipeline reimplements a mechanism the winning system already used. What is new is knowing where the score comes from: every stage is measured alone, and every metric is broken down by how often the heading appeared in training.

  • knnSubjects of the 20 nearest indexed documents, summed by similarity. Reaches headings some record carries — 0.69 R@10 on head, 0.000 on zero-shot.
  • denseThe record scored against all 79,427 vocabulary entries rendered as bilingual text. Reaches any heading with a name; the band ordering is inverted against kNN.
  • lexicalHeading strings matched verbatim against title and abstract. Nearly flat across all four bands at 0.14–0.19.
  • fusionReciprocal rank fusion over the three, because they score in incomparable units. 100 candidates out, 50 submitted.
  • rerankerA cross-encoder rereads each pair and its order is fused with the retrieval order, not substituted: replacing loses 0.06 R@10 by wrecking the head band.
  • fine-tuneLoRA contrastive training on one A100, 87 minutes: +0.0668 dev R@10, against +0.0079 for doubling the corpus. It buys the head and sells the zero-shot band.

Two disciplines every number rests on

The model cutoff

Every model any stage loads is pinned to a revision released on or before 2025-01-31, the close of the evaluation window, so a comparison against teams who competed in January 2025 is not flattered by a year of model progress. A name does not carry that claim — both E5 checkpoints this project started from had commits landed on them in April 2026 — so a committed registry records each model's creation date, and an unvouched model is refused before any weights are fetched.

The split, opened once

Every configuration decision was made on 5,354 dev records. The gold split was read on 2026-09-09 under a configuration digested into a plan file and committed one commit earlier; the run refuses a row the plan does not name or whose digest has moved, and the receipt written afterwards makes a second read a refusal that has to carry a recorded justification.

One caveat is reported because the spec committed to reporting it: 134 test records duplicate an indexed training document's title and abstract under a different TIBKAT id, which a neighbour retriever finds unusually easy. Removing them moves R@10 by 0.0017.
