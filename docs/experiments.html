long tail

What each experiment establishes about the tail

Labels appearing in 1–9 training documents. Too rare for a neighbour harvest to surface reliably, too frequent to be a zero-shot case, and 75.2% of gold assignments live in the torso and tail together.

  • rung1-knn Tail (4,177 gold assignments) is the weakest band the retriever reaches at all: micro recall 0.0493 at R@5, 0.1089 at R@10, 0.1678 at R@25, 0.2052 at R@50. The section states this as the head-to-tail spread — "0.69 against 0.11 at k=10. A single averaged number hides a factor of six." — and keeps tail separate from zero (1,117 assignments, 0.0000 at every k), which it calls unreachable because a label in no indexed document "cannot be harvested from a neighbour at any k, at any index size", 8.5% of dev gold assignments.
  • rung1-dense The section prints a complete tail row — 4,177 dev gold assignments, micro recall 0.1283 at k=5, 0.1628 at k=10, 0.2195 at k=25, 0.2667 at k=50, against rung1-knn's 0.1089 at k=10 — but makes no tail-specific claim about it. Its stated result is that the whole band ordering is "inverted against kNN, monotonically": best where the neighbour harvest has nothing (zero, quoted in prose as 0.26 against 0.00) and worst where the harvest is strongest (head, 0.05 against 0.69), with the zero row carrying the explicit ticket 05 argument and the two opposite failure directions motivating the fusion ticket 07 measures. Tail is the one band besides zero whose printed tower figure exceeds the printed kNN figure (0.1628 against 0.1089), but the section neither singles the tail out nor quotes any ratio between the two.
  • rung1-lexical The tail (4,177 gold assignments) is flat with the rest of the lexical profile — R@5 0.1053, R@10 0.1456, R@25 0.2088, R@50 0.2425, against torso's 0.1445 at k=10 and inside the section's stated span of "0.14 to 0.19" — because, in the section's words, "whether a heading's name appears in a title has nothing to do with how many training records carry it, so frequency is not a variable this mechanism responds to". At k=10 lexical's tail 0.1456 is above kNN's 0.1089 and below the label tower's 0.1628, but the tail is not the only band where lexical beats kNN: it also beats it on zero (0.1925 against a bolded 0.0000), and the section names the head (0.1886) and the zero-shot band (0.1925), not the tail, as lexical's own two extremes.
  • rung1-fused At k=10 the fused row scores tail 0.2262, quoted by the section as the third of fusion's three margins — "tail 0.2262 against the tower's 0.1628" — with kNN alone at 0.1089, lexical alone at 0.1456, dense+knn at 0.1463 and knn+lexical at 0.1365; the claim is explicitly scoped to single retrievers ("above every single retriever in three of the four bands"), and the printed table has the kNN-free pair dense+lexical at 0.2361, above the fused 0.2262, which the section never mentions. The mechanism it gives is that the three retrievers fail in different places — "kNN monotonically down the bands, the tower monotonically up, lexical flat" — and that "combining them recovers most of each". At the candidate ceiling (k=100) the fused set holds the right label for "46.5% of tail" assignments, 0.4654 against kNN's 0.2449, and the section names tail and zero as "where the other two retrievers do their work"; that ceiling table is what the later stages are budgeted against, and nothing downstream can exceed 0.4654 in this band "without a different candidate generator" — though the section attaches the reranker (ticket 12) and the group prior (ticket 11) to the zero band's 0.1871, not to the tail.
  • baseline The tail band (4,177 dev gold assignments, each tail label seen in 1-9 training documents) is exactly 0.0000 at every k the section prints - R@5 0.0000, R@10 0.0000, R@25 0.0000, R@50 0.0000 - so unlike torso, which reaches 0.0036 at k=25 and 0.0160 at k=50, tail never becomes non-zero out to k=50. The section says the torso and tail zeros "were not predicted in advance and are the more damaging finding" because the two bands carry 76% of dev's gold assignments between them: a 14,607-way sigmoid head trained with BCE on 78,037 assignments, against a per-column negative rate above 99.98%, collapses onto the frequent columns, emitting 78 of 14,607 distinct codes anywhere in 5,354 x 50 slots and 26 in any top-10, with 97.4% of top-50 slots occupied by head-band labels and 2.6% by torso-band labels.
  • rung1-bilingual Under bilingual label text the tail band (4,177 gold assignments) is where the two label-reading retrievers move least and most: the dense label tower sits at 0.1597 R@10 with Δ −0.0031, the smallest of its three band losses, because "the tower's loss is monotone in frequency and lands almost entirely on the head". The lexical retriever sits at 0.1858 R@10 with Δ +0.0402, since "the lexical gains run the other way, biggest on tail and zero-shot: a specific compound or named entity is exactly the kind of heading whose English name appears verbatim in an English abstract"; the section prints no German-only tail absolute for either retriever.
  • rung1-prior The tail band (4,177 gold assignments) gains from the group-prior boost, but only slightly: micro R@10 goes from 0.2612 unboosted to 0.2648 boosted at the committed weight 0.5, +0.0036. It is one of the "three of the four bands" that "gain, in descending order of label frequency" (head +0.0158, torso +0.0041, tail +0.0036), so the tail is not the band that pays — the zero-shot band is, losing 0.0018, or 2 gold assignments of 1,117. The section's verdict is nevertheless that "the prior is a head-band instrument": it buys 1.6 points of the head band and gives back a fifth of a point of the zero band, and the section says nothing further about the tail.
  • rung1-rerank The tail gains under both mixing modes, and it is the band that gains most once the two orders are fused. On 200 tail assignments in the 300-record dev sample, fused R@10 is 0.2900; replacing the fused order with the cross-encoder's raises it to 0.3450 (+0.0550) while the head pays 0.2825, and fusing at mix_weight 1.0 raises it to 0.3700 (+0.0800), the largest gain of any band in the fused-with-reranker table, against head -0.0458, torso +0.0343 and zero +0.0509. Beyond those two table rows the section's prose says nothing about the tail: its headline for the replacement table is the zero band (+0.1526, "the largest single-band movement any component has produced"), and the explanation that a band is "reachable only through label text, which is the one thing a cross-encoder reads carefully" is made about the zero-shot band, not the tail. The only prose that covers the tail's movement is the general one that the fused band breakdown "keeps most of what replacement bought and gives back most of what it cost".
  • rung1-encoders The tail is where the encoder choice pays: gte-multilingual-base is "+0.0699 on tail against e5-base" (0.3311 vs 0.2612) while being only -0.0108 on head, and the section frames the 4,132 torso and tail labels carrying 75.2% of assignments as "where the four models actually differ", against 65 head labels carrying 16.0% and already at 0.69-0.71 whatever the encoder. The aggregate winner does not take the tail — bge-m3 has the best tail at 0.3361 — and within the E5 family the larger checkpoint is the worst tail row at 0.2387 against its base's 0.2612; the section does not explain that tail gap by the tower, saying only in general that "because the label tower is what reaches the tail, the fused number followed the tower rather than the neighbours" (dense 0.0852 against 0.1149), and reserving its explicit band-level tower argument for the zero band, where bge-m3's 0.2498 and its dense 0.2375 against 0.1999 are called "the same fact".
  • rung2 The section's headline is that "A fourfold index buys the tail": going from 8,000 to 32,043 indexed documents raises tail recall by +0.1240 for bge-m3 (0.3361 to 0.4601), +0.1271 for gte-multilingual-base (0.3311 to 0.4582), and +0.1202 and +0.1360 for the E5 pair (multilingual-e5-base 0.2612 to 0.3814; multilingual-e5-large 0.2387 to 0.3747) — against only +0.0084 and −0.0119 on the head for the top two, because the head is already near its ceiling whatever the pipeline does and the 4,132 torso and tail labels carry 75.2% of assignments. The tail is also where the encoders differ most, which the section uses to explain why the official macro-over-cells aggregation (spread 0.0116) barely separates them while micro spreads them over 0.0411: the cells the official average weights up are small ones whose labels are not tail-heavy. The tail gain comes partly at the zero-shot band's expense, which goes down for every encoder by 0.0054 to 0.0152 — displacement rather than loss, since zero-shot recall at 100 is unchanged to four decimals for three of the four encoders (0.5184 bge-m3, 0.4942 gte, 0.4584 multilingual-e5-base) and moves only 0.0009 for multilingual-e5-large (0.4288 to 0.4297).
  • rung3 Growing the index from 32,043 to 70,579 documents lifts the tail band from 0.4601 to 0.4719 (bge-m3) and 0.4582 to 0.4733 (gte-multilingual-base) — the section prints no delta for that step, quoting deltas only for the zero band (+0.0223, +0.0188) — while fine-tuning at the fixed 70,579 index leaves it nearly still: "the tail barely moves (+0.02, +0.03)", printed as +0.0160 (0.4719 to 0.4879) and +0.0326 (0.4733 to 0.5059), the smallest gain of any band under a fine-tune the section calls "monotonic in label frequency" (head +0.0968/+0.1279, torso +0.0784/+0.0876, zero −0.0564/−0.0233). Separately, and without changing any frozen band assignment, "All 173" formerly-zero dev labels the larger corpus reaches "land in the tail band", not one in torso or head: they "appear in one to nine of the 70,579 documents, so kNN can propose them in principle and will rarely rank them", and "the +0.02 the zero band gained is what 173 barely-present labels are worth".
  • test The tail band holds 3,622 gold assignments (30.7% of the split's 11,798 gold assignments, not of its records) and scores 0.5353 test R@10, against 0.5059 in the same encoder's rung-3 dev column without the cross-encoder, printed as a change of +0.0294 — one of the two bands the table bolds as improved, the other being zero at +0.1380, while head shows −0.0869 and torso −0.0496. The section attaches no causal reading to the tail specifically: its interpretive sentence says the shape ticket 12 measured on a 300-record dev sample is that "the cross-encoder gains on the zero-shot band and loses on the head", and it warns that the whole right-hand column is confounded twice (different split, different pipeline) and is offered "as a reading rather than a measurement". Separately it notes the band supports land within 0.1pp of the shares docs/spec.md froze the bands at.

precision

Precision, and the ceiling that bounds it

Precision at a fixed k is capped by how many labels a book actually has. At 2.44 labels per dev book, a perfect system scores at most 0.4616 P@5 and 0.2434 P@10 — the other slots are forced wrong. So a raw precision figure says little; the share of achievable precision beside it says what the system did with the slots it could win. The of max columns are that share.

Where precision is not reported at all

  • rung1-bilingual, rung1-prior, rung1-encoders, rung2, rung3 report no precision figure. Those rungs — bilingual label text, the group prior, the four-encoder screen, rung 2 and rung 3 — were selected on micro Recall@10 and report recall and band breakdowns only.
  • That is a real gap rather than a presentational one: an encoder that bought recall by spending precision would not have been caught by the screen that chose it.

The finding

This section derives, from three committed inputs and no run at all, that 812 of the subject headings the gold test split assigns appear on no document anywhere, carrying 851 gold assignments (7.2%) that any neighbour-harvest method on the tib-core-subjects leaderboard cannot reach at any k. Against those same 851 assignments the single test run's submission tree scores 0.323 R@10 by reading the label's name rather than harvesting neighbours, while the rejected closed-vocabulary classifier scores 0.0000 there structurally. Dev corroborates the bound at 880 of 1,053 zero-shot headings unreachable, carrying 7.1% of dev gold assignments.

What it decided

  • The model cutoff. Every model any stage loads is released on or before 2025-01-31, the close of the SemEval-2025 evaluation window, so the comparison against teams who competed in January 2025 is not flattered by a year of model progress. A name does not carry that claim — both E5 checkpoints this project started from had commits landed on them in April 2026 — so reference/model_releases.json records each model's creation date and pins the newest revision inside the cutoff, verify_model_releases.py re-derives it, and models.check_cutoff refuses an unvouched model before any weights are fetched.
  • The test split, opened once. Every configuration decision in this document was made on the 5,354 dev records. The gold split was read on 2026-09-09, under a configuration digested into reference/test_plan.json and committed one commit earlier (d1109ec); the run refuses a row the plan does not name or whose digest has moved, and reference/test_run.json is the receipt that makes a second read a refusal rather than a decision. The bound above is derived from that same opening and refuses to run before it — llms4subjects.testset.require_read, because a derivation that opened the split itself would be the read.
  • Model selection is micro Recall@10 on dev, and the official macro-over-cells figure is reported beside it because that is what the leaderboard is quoted on.
  • Read the gap against the aggregation before reading it against the method: the aggregation alone moves the figure by +0.1245 at k=10, where 0.08 separates the best published R@10 from the worst of the four.
  • Reaching the unreachable assignments takes a retriever that scores a document against the text of all 79,427 vocabulary entries (ticket 05), bilingual label names so an English document can match a German heading (ticket 08), and a cross-encoder that re-reads the pair (ticket 12) — which on test bought back more than fine-tuning the retriever had sold.
  • The rejected approach is kept runnable as the reference point: bert-base-multilingual-cased with a dense layer over the 14,607 training labels scores 0.0667 dev micro R@10 against the pipeline's 0.6044, measured through the same evaluator and the same frozen bands as every other row.

Caveats and what did not run

  • The bound is a property of the data, not of a model: it is derived from three committed things and no run at all (reference/frequency_bands.json is read rather than recomputed).
  • The 180 headings the larger corpus does reach all land in the tail band — one to nine documents each — so reaching them and retrieving them are different things; rung 3 measured what they were worth at +0.02 on the zero-shot band.
  • A neighbour harvest scores 0.000 on the 851 unreachable assignments by construction, and a closed-vocabulary classifier cannot represent them at all — these are structural facts, not measurements.
  • The rejected baseline scores 0.0000 on the zero-shot band at every k structurally, since its output layer has no column for a label core_train never used.
  • Eleven of the twenty cells this project can score are cells the organizers' scorer cannot read at all: the 0.6299 leaderboard R@10 is over the 9 cells their scorer can read, against 0.7156 at the one-vote-per-cell aggregation over all 20 cells and 0.5910 record-micro.
  • The precision/recall split is confounded by record length: 34.1% of the test split carries exactly one gold heading, where this system reaches 0.7367 R@10, against 0.4163 on records with five or more — the aggregate the leaderboard is read on rewards finding a correct heading over finding all of them.
  • The test split was opened once, on 2026-09-09; the run refuses a row the plan does not name or whose digest has moved, and the receipt in reference/test_run.json makes a second read a refusal rather than a decision.
  • A model name does not carry the cutoff claim — both E5 checkpoints this project started from had commits landed on them in April 2026 — so release dates are recorded and revisions pinned rather than inferred.
  • Every number in the document is dev except in two sections — "The finding" and "The test set, opened once" — which are the single test run.
  • The two zero-shot rows together are the frozen zero-shot band the test section reports at 0.3547: 275 hits of 851 plus 96 of 195 is 371 of 1,046.
